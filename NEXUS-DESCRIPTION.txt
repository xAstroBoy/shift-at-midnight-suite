
[size=5][b]Shift At Midnight Suite[/b][/size]

One mod that replaces the whole scattered stack of Shift At Midnight cheat and
QoL mods, fixes the bugs they shipped with, and adds a great deal more.

Press [b]F1[/b] for a real mouse-driven panel: click a category in the sidebar,
tick checkboxes, click the -/+ steppers, type in the search boxes. Drag the
title bar to move it, drag an edge to resize it. UnityExplorer keeps F7 and is
left completely untouched.

[size=4][b]What's in it - 11 pages[/b][/size]

[b]PLAYER[/b]
Infinite stamina. God mode. Infinite money - all three wallets, meaning the
store balance, arcade tokens, and story mode's personal funds (the one the vet's
bills come out of). Wallet floor. Infinite store refreshes. Infinite ammo with
an adjustable ammo ceiling. Freeze consumables. Inventory slots up to 8, and you
can write the number permanently to your save. Move speed and jump height
multipliers. No-clip with speed control.

[b]COMFORT[/b]
Max customer patience with a patience allowance. Customers always happy. Honest
stock rating. Remove store curbs, chain fences, and the forest roof. Silence
bells. Auto-unlock items after talking. Humans invincible. Instant traps and
boarding. Auto-fuel cars. Vents don't kick you out. No hint popups. Instant
emoti-scope, and reveal the anomaly-lens objects without the lens. No fog,
fullbright, brightness boost. Kill extra monsters, kill baby dolls only, skip the
entity countdown and the hunt briefing - each manual or automatic. Fast customer
report with an adjustable speed.

[b]SPAWNER[/b]
Every item in the game, searchable. Set the amount, the item storage (ammo it
arrives carrying), and the spawn method - Auto, Drop, Throw, or Local.

[b]WEAPONS[/b]
Spawn pre-loaded, networked weapons. Plus a grenade-launcher mode for the
flamethrower, with fire rate, grenades per burst, and projectile speed.

[b]PLAYERS[/b]
A per-player target list, and a full toolkit for each.
Hurt: damage, down, kill, stick them in place, sic monsters on them, max scent.
Help: heal to max, revive, respawn, clear scent.
Move: teleport them to you, you to them, or them to the store.
Inventory: drop everything, explode held item, jam or unjam gun, pulverize, set
their slot count.
Misc: rename, hunt light on/off.

[b]DOPPELS[/b]
Spawn 1, 3, or the game's own eight-doppelganger horde. Detector that warns when
you look at one, radar that counts every one nearby, on-screen detection
evidence, corner badge, adjustable warning text size.

[b]CREATURES[/b]
Click-to-spawn pickers for monsters and bosses, scripted customers with ID
cards, ambient shoppers, and pets - plus toggleable set pieces (cow, dentists,
Pubert, and the rest).

[b]EVENTS[/b]
Every entry in the game's event atlas, click to run it.

[b]RPC[/b]
Every networked call in the game, reflected off every NetworkBehaviour,
searchable and clickable. [cmd] entries are client-to-host commands; the rest
need state authority.

[b]STORE[/b]
Auto-bag items, allow unbagged checkout, non-doppels self-checkout, clear
counter leftovers, complete transaction now. Auto-stock shelves. Auto-clean
spills, moppables, and trash.

[b]WORLD[/b]
Unlock the full arsenal, weapon wall always open, gun case for everyone. Skip
forced text reveal, finish current objective. UNSTICK ME (F2) with an
auto-unstick on hunt start. Endless Night, plus Call The Bus. Freeze or advance
the shift clock. Hunt without entities, block hunts entirely, send entities away,
force end hunt. Block rake spawns. Multiply customers, events, nuisances, and
doppelgangers. Always perfect reviews. Unlock all hats and cosmetics. UI scale.
Log dumps and a profiler.

[size=4][b]Endless Night[/b][/size]

"Your shift is done. Clean up any messes and head to the bus" is what the clock
reaching zero buys you, and it is not just a banner: the store stops generating,
the shoppers walk out, and you are stood in a finished night waiting to be allowed
to leave.

Endless Night lets the clock keep [i]running[/i] and winds it back before it can
expire, so time still moves and the generation scheduled against it keeps firing.
Browsing customers are held open too, so a long night stays a busy one instead of
an empty store with a timer.

Nothing about it blocks the day from completing. That is deliberate - patching the
ending out would mean boarding the bus did nothing and you were stuck in a night
you could not leave. The night simply never reaches its own end, and
[b]CALL THE BUS[/b] brings the bus in whenever you want to go. Board it and the
night ends exactly as it always did.

[size=4][b]Bugs it fixes in the mods it replaces[/b][/size]

[b]The item spawner refused to spawn most items.[/b] The old trainer scanned the
loaded scene for a PickupObject whose objectIndex matched and gave up if none
existed - so anything not already lying around that night could not be spawned -
and its item list was a hardcoded 52 of 59 ids. The real catalogue is
StoreManager.pickupObjs, a prefab array indexed by item id. The spawner reads
that now, so every id in the game is spawnable and the list length comes from
the real array.

[b]Spawned items were local ghosts.[/b] Spawning went through Object.Instantiate,
which no other player ever saw. It now goes through the game's own networked
path, Rpc_CMD_NetworkDropObject, so everyone sees what you spawn. Direct
instantiation is the offline fallback only.

[b]Spawned grenades exploded on landing.[/b] StoreManager keeps two prefab arrays
- pickupObjs (inert) and thrownObjs (armed) - and the old version spawned through
ServerThrowObject, the throw path, which arms explosives by design. Drop
semantics are the default now and explosives never go down the throw path, not
even as a fallback. Items are classified by their real prefab name rather than a
hardcoded id table, since the inherited id list is not guaranteed to match this
build's array order.

[b]Infinite ammo did nothing for spawned guns.[/b] Two causes. Spawned weapons got
itemStorage = 0, so they arrived empty. And the cheat only held each slot at the
highest value it had ever seen, so a gun that arrives at 0 has a high-water mark
of 0 and never refills. Storage is automatic now and gives weapons a full load,
and weapon slots are topped up to a target instead. Only slots holding an actual
weapon are touched, because itemStorages also carries crate contents and forcing
those to 99 would break stocking.

[b]Auto-clean left most of the mess.[/b] The old check required the object to be
named exactly "BloodSpill(Clone)" and tagged Mess, so bathroom blots, vomit and
oil were all skipped - and the Moppable component was never touched at all, even
though it is what most floor mess actually uses. It now cleans every Spill and
every Moppable regardless of name, plus loose Trash. A failed clean is retried
after three seconds instead of blacklisting the object for the rest of the night.

[b]Auto-stock wedged on shelves it could not finish.[/b] It derived a per-slot cap
of maxProductsOnShelf / slotCount; when that did not divide evenly - 10 across 3
slots caps at 9 - every slot read "full" while the shelf was still incomplete,
and stocking stalled forever. It now works off the shelf's own productsOnShelf
against maxProductsOnShelf and fills the emptiest slot.

[b]Auto-stock needed a crate in hand.[/b] It only armed from a Harmony hook on
crate pickup. It polls instead now and by default needs no crate at all - turn on
"Only Stock From Held Crate" for the old behaviour.

[b]The gun case patch targeted the wrong type.[/b] The old BepInEx plugin patched
GunCase.Interact, but GunCase does not override Interact - it inherits it from
Interactable, so the patch never applied. It now sits on Interactable and filters
for gun cases, backed by a once-a-second sweep that also catches cases spawned
later.

[size=4][b]Fixed in the current build[/b][/size]

[b]Humans Invincible was shielding people it should not have.[/b] Norbert, the
robbers and the antler man all wear a customer's scripts, so they matched the
browse and dialogue tests and became unkillable. The first attempt at a fix read
the game's own flags - dontPunishForKilling, hasBounty, damageToPlayer - on the
theory that those mark something you are meant to kill. They do not: Clyde Dawson
and the car NPCs carry enough of them to be let through, and both are ordinary
people. So the decision is by name, from an editable list (HostileNames in
MelonPreferences.cfg), which is narrow and predictable. Anything the shield does
protect prints a SHIELDED: line naming it, so if you find someone who should be
killable you can add them yourself without waiting for a patch. Nuisances are
classified before any of it, so "wounded, never killed" still holds for them.
A dead test that looked for an Npc component came out: Npc is a ScriptableObject,
so it could never have matched anything, and every line in the log agreed.

[b]Event objectives went uncredited when the mod cleaned up.[/b] Cleaning a
Moppable through Rpc_CMD_Clean removes the roach but never tells the counter, so
the roach infestation objective sat unfinished while the floor came up spotless.
Roaches are now counted as they are cleaned, once each, and the rat infestation
gets the same treatment.

[b]No Hunt Briefing.[/b] Skipping the entity countdown brought the entity in early
but left the explanation panels up with their own timer, which is what you were
actually sat waiting on. A new toggle closes them the moment they appear.

[b]Instant Traps missed often enough to notice.[/b] Three separate causes. The fill
bar was only pushed when InventoryManager.tasking was set, and not every
hold-to-do sets it. Boarding a door was "fixed" by writing the barricade timer up
to its delay, which only helps if that timer counts up - if it counts down, that
write restarted the wait. And completing the bar from a Harmony hook races the
game's own Update either way. So the hold requirement is removed at source
instead: Interactable.holdInteractableTime is zeroed on whatever you are looking
at, and put back the moment you look away.

[b]Cleanup caused lag spikes on a night with a lot of limbs.[/b] Spills, Moppables
and Trash were all swept in the same tick - three FindObjectsOfType calls, each
walking the scene, landing in one frame twice a second. The Trash sweep alone is
large once the floor is covered, and sharing a frame with the other two is what
turned a cost into a stutter. They rotate now, one type per pass, for the same
throughput spread out.

[b]The menu did not block item use.[/b] Clicking a checkbox was also pulling the
trigger, because the menu is an overlay and the game never learns a click was
meant for something else. Opening it now takes the game's own item lock, and only
releases it if the menu is what took it - a conversation holding the lock keeps
holding it.

[size=4][b]Other things it stops doing to you[/b][/size]

[b]The end-of-day report crawls.[/b] The customer report deals its folders out one
at a time and the money counter ticks its way up, each on its own timer. Fast
Customer Report raises the clock only while that screen is up - restoring whatever
it replaced - and asks the report for the next card directly, since the pacing is
not necessarily on a scaled timer. The figures are untouched: this changes how
long they take to be shown, not what they say.

[b]Hint popups.[/b] "Remember to...", "ask the driver...", all of it. They funnel
through StoreManager.AddHint, and that is refused outright, so nothing is ever
queued and nothing ever appears. On by default.

[b]Vents throw you out.[/b] VentTrigger drives that with a push-out animator.
Switching it off leaves getting in and out working exactly as before - the vent
just stops deciding you have been in there long enough.

[b]The emoti-scope takes its time.[/b] Instant Emoti-Scope pushes the scan progress
rather than forcing the result, so the game's own threshold fires and the emotion
reads out normally, only sooner.

[b]The anomaly lens.[/b] Reveal Anomaly-Lens Objects switches off the ScanOnlyObject
masking so the second figure on a customer is visible without holding the lens.
The jumpscare is excluded on purpose - revealing that one means walking around
with it permanently in shot.

[b]Extra inventory slots were built but unusable.[/b] The HUD widget for a new slot
was cloned from the Animator's GameObject, then the two Images were looked for
underneath it - an assumption that only holds if the sprites are children of the
animator, and in this game they are not. The clone bailed out, silently, and the
request was clamped back to four while the log cheerfully reported "Inventory slots
set to 4". It now clones the smallest object containing the animator and both
images, whatever the hierarchy looks like, grows the per-slot text arrays as well,
repairs itself if a network resync shortens what it built, and says out loud why if
it still cannot.

[size=4][b]Multiplayer[/b][/size]

Rows marked [i]host only[/i] are greyed out when you do not have state authority.
Item spawning works either way: as host you spawn directly, as a client you ask
the host.

[size=4][b]Installation[/b][/size]

[list=1]
[*]Install MelonLoader 0.7.3 (IL2CPP / net6).
[*]Put [b]ShiftAtMidnightSuite.Loader.dll[/b] in [b]Mods\[/b]
[*]Put [b]ShiftAtMidnightSuite.Plugin.dll[/b] in [b]UserData\ShiftAtMidnightSuite\[/b]
[*]Put [b]UniverseLib.ML.IL2CPP.Interop.dll[/b] in [b]UserLibs\[/b]
[/list]

The plugin goes under UserData rather than Mods on purpose - if MelonLoader saw
it in Mods it would load it as a mod itself, lock the file, and put its types in
the wrong context.

[line]

[size=4][b]Why it is split into two DLLs[/b][/size]

MelonLoader loads mods into a context .NET cannot unload, so anything that is a
mod needs a game restart to change. The loader is therefore deliberately tiny
and stable, and the entire feature set lives in a plugin loaded into a
collectible AssemblyLoadContext. [b]F3[/b] hot-reloads it without restarting the
game.

[line]

[size=4][b]Configuration[/b][/size]

Settings persist to [b]UserData\MelonPreferences.cfg[/b] under
[b][ShiftAtMidnightSuite][/b]. Rebind the menu to any UnityEngine.KeyCode name
(F4, Insert, Backspace, ...) with the [b]MenuKey[/b] entry.

UniverseLib initialises a second or two after the game loads. If you press F1
before then - or if UniverseLib is unavailable - a plain keyboard-driven fallback
menu opens instead, and the log tells you which one you got.
